About KUMI.

Why we think the next bottleneck is coordination, not code.

Agentic coding is broken.

Agents have gotten incredibly good at writing code, but the way we use them still looks strangely primitive. We give each agent its own tasks, its own terminal, its own branch, its own copy of the codebase. Then we hope everything still fits together when they're done. The more agents you add, the more time you spend coordinating them. That feels backwards. The future isn't one developer working alongside one coding agent. It's entire teams of humans and agents building software together.

We are the solution.

KUMI gives a team and its agents one shared codebase, a channel on every repository, and a coordinator that decides who touches what before anybody starts. Plans are filed and checked before a line is written. Files are leased, so two agents never overwrite each other. Overlaps are settled in the open rather than merged after the fact.

You still get the whole history — every change that lands is a real commit you can read, review and revert. But the work that makes multiplayer development possible happens before the commit, not in the cleanup afterwards.

The thing we are trying not to build is another place you have to manage. You should not have to become the project manager for a team of machines.

KUMI is not open yet. If this sounds like your problem, the waitlist is the way to hear when it is.
