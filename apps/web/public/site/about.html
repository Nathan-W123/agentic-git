Why we think the next bottleneck is coordination, not code.

Agents have gotten remarkably good at writing code. The way we use them has not kept up. Each one gets its own task, its own terminal, its own branch, its own copy of the codebase, and we hope it all still fits together at the end. The more agents you add, the more of your day goes on keeping them out of each other's way.

That is a coordination problem, and it is the one we are working on. KUMI gives a team and its agents one shared codebase, a channel on every repository, and a coordinator that decides who touches what before anybody starts. Plans are filed and checked. Files are leased. Overlaps are settled rather than merged. Every change that lands is an ordinary Git commit, with the history to read and revert like any other.

The thing we are trying not to build is another place you have to manage. You should not have to become the project manager for a team of machines.

KUMI is not open yet. If that sounds like your problem, the waitlist is the way to hear when it is.
